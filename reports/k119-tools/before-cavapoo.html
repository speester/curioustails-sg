What is a Cavapoo?

A Cavapoo, also called a Cavoodle, is a cross between a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel and a Toy Poodle. It is a small companion dog with a wavy, low-shedding coat, an affectionate and intelligent temperament, moderate exercise needs of 30 to 60 minutes a day, and a lifespan of 12 to 15 years.

Cavapoo Temperament: What They’re Really Like at Home

Cavapoo puppies in Singapore homes are affectionate, loyal, intelligent, and playful; that part every seller will tell you. What matters more for your decision is this: a Cavapoo is noticeably more independent than the breeds it usually gets compared against, and that changes which homes it suits.

Nelson's honest note from two years of matching puppies to families: the single most common mismatch we see is a working couple choosing a companion breed that needs company all day. Maltipoos need more daily human contact; Cavapoos cope better on their own. That one difference decides more happy outcomes than any coat colour or price tag.

They're gentle with children, sociable with other dogs, and moderate barkers. A bored Cavapoo will let you know it's bored, but a settled routine keeps them calm in an apartment.

Can a Cavapoo Be Left Alone During Work Hours?

Can Cavapoos be left alone for a workday? Yes, better than most toy crosses, provided the routine is set up properly from day one. Cavapoos handle a working couple's 8am-to-6pm absence more comfortably than a Maltipoo or Bichonpoo, which is exactly why we often steer working households toward them.

The playpen matters as much as the breed. A puppy that treats its playpen as home territory, with its own bed, water, toys, and toilet area, settles into alone time instead of fretting through it. That's the setup we build in your home on delivery day, before your Cavapoo ever walks in.

If nobody is home all day AND you want a dog that thrives on constant company, tell us honestly. We'd rather talk you out of the wrong breed than sell you the wrong dog.

A Cavapoo is an HDB-approved small breed, so you can keep one in an HDB flat. HDB allows one dog of an approved breed per flat, and the Cavapoo's compact 3 to 10 kg size sits comfortably within the small-breed rules. Daily Life with a Cavapoo in Singapore

A Cavapoo's exercise needs are moderate: 30 to 60 minutes a day, split into a morning and an evening walk around the heat; midday pavement here is genuinely too hot for small paws. Shaded park connectors and void-deck-adjacent green spaces do the job; a Cavapoo doesn't need a landed garden to be happy.

Rainy weeks and haze days happen, and this is where the breed's size helps: a corridor of indoor fetch, ten minutes of trick training, and a stuffed chew toy will cover an off day without a restless dog by evening.

Noise-wise, neighbours generally won't have complaints. Cavapoos bark to announce, not to protest all day, provided the alone-time routine is settled early.

Cavapoo Grooming and Coat Care in Our Humid Climate

Brush 3 to 4 times a week; that wavy, low-shedding coat mats faster in Singapore's humidity than overseas care guides suggest. Book professional grooming every 6 to 8 weeks to keep the coat manageable and nails in check.

Those soft floppy ears trap moisture here, so make a quick weekly ear check part of the routine; it's the cheapest ear-infection prevention there is. The brush and grooming tools you need for the first months are already in the starter kit.

Cavapoo Health: What We Check Before You Take One Home

Every Cavapoo goes home vet-checked, vaccinated, dewormed, and microchipped. The Cavapoo health issues worth knowing are three: ear infections from those floppy ears, inherited eye conditions, and patellar luxation, a kneecap issue common to small breeds. We say this once, plainly, because you should hear it from your seller and not from a forum at midnight.

Feeding is simple: your puppy comes with the small-breed food it's already eating, and the transition to any new brand should be gradual over a week or so. For portioning and feeding-stage guidance, small-breed puppy feeding guidance is a reliable reference, and our puppy health checks explained covers what we screen and why.
