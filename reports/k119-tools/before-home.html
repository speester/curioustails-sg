What is an AVS-licensed puppy shop?

An AVS-licensed pet shop is a Singapore business approved by the Animal and Veterinary Service, a unit of the National Parks Board, to keep and sell dogs. Licensing means welfare inspections, mandatory owner-suitability screening, and annual renewal, not just a name on a shopfront.

Curious Tails holds AVS pet shop licence AS24J00046. You can check ours, or any shop's, on the public registry below.

Search the AVS public registry

What licensing actually requires

  • Regular welfare inspections of how animals are kept and sold
  • Mandatory owner-suitability screening before a sale (the Pet Purchase Declaration)
  • Annual licence renewal, not a one-time approval

A shop with none of this is unlicensed, regardless of what it claims.

Bringing a Puppy Home to an HDB Flat

HDB rules allow one dog of an approved breed, or an approved cross, per flat. Condo and landed property owners can keep up to three, subject to the property's own pet policy.

Corgis and Shiba Inus are not on the standard HDB-approved list. We'll tell you this plainly if you're considering either, rather than let you find out after you've already fallen for a puppy.

Cavapoo, Maltipoo, Maltese, Bichonpoo, Bichon Frise, Cavapoochon, Chihuahua, Cockapoo, and Mini Dachshund are commonly approved as small breeds or crosses, though it's worth checking the full HDB-approved breed list and arranging dog licensing with us before you commit.

How Every Puppy Is Checked Before You Take It Home

Every puppy is health-checked before going home, and vaccinated in line with the Singapore Vaccination Guidelines for Dogs and Cats, jointly published by AVS and the Singapore Veterinary Association. Core vaccination protects against canine parvovirus and distemper, two of the most serious risks to a young puppy.

We mention this once, plainly, because it matters, not because we want you worried every time you read our site.

Verifying Our AVS Licence

Curious Tails is AVS-licensed under pet shop licence AS24J00046. You can check this yourself, in under a minute, on the AVS public registry.

This matters because not every seller in Singapore is licensed. Puppies from unlicensed sources are often smuggled in from across the border, without health checks or proper vaccination, and are often listed on Carousell or Telegram by an unlicensed pet seller. If a seller can't show you a licence number you can verify on the public registry, that's worth pausing on. An honest pet shop will have one; a genuine puppy seller won't mind you checking.

Beyond the licence, the questions worth asking are whether the price is all-in or has vaccination, microchipping and licensing bolted on afterwards, whether you can visit the puppy in person, and whether anyone answers the phone a month after you pay. 11How much does a puppy typically cost in Singapore?

Expect roughly $3,000 to $8,000 from a licensed shop, depending on the breed. 12What should I look for in a dog shop in Singapore?

Four things, in this order: a verifiable AVS licence number, a physical shop you can actually walk into, one published all-in price rather than a headline figure plus extras, and honesty about which breeds are not HDB-approved. A shop that tells you a Corgi cannot go into a flat before you fall for the photo is a shop that will tell you the rest of the truth too. 14Are your puppies SG-born, or imported?

Both, and the listing says which. Every puppy carries its country of origin on its card, most commonly Australia, alongside its age and vaccination status. Imported puppies enter Singapore through the licensed AVS import process with the paperwork to match; that process is the entire difference between a legally imported puppy and a smuggled one, and it is the reason a licensed puppy costs more than a Telegram listing.
