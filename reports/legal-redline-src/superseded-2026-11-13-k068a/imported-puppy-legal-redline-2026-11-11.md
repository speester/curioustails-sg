## What this document is

Curious Tails plans to list puppies that are with breeders in Ireland as "Available for reservation · Imported". The buyer pays a 50% deposit to start the import and the 50% balance before the puppy flies. The price is fixed and all-in, delivered to the buyer's door. Listings are found by an automated feed of Irish classified sites and published only after owner approval. The supplier is not named on the website; the buyer learns the breeder's identity in the written reservation confirmation. The same terms also govern the Australian reservations the live site already takes (clause A1).

The project team wrote first-draft refund, arrival and takedown wording inside its spec and copy deck (2026-11-08). This document redlines that wording into three instruments for your review:

- Part A. Reservation terms (the contract the buyer receives with the written reservation confirmation).
- Part B. Delay clause (arrival estimate, notice, cancellation right and long-stop date).
- Part C. Takedown policy (public notice plus the internal procedure).

Part D lists the customer-facing copy that must change to match. Part E lists the questions we need you to answer. Deleted base text is struck through; new text is underlined (tracked changes in the Word file). Comments addressed to counsel sit beside each clause. Items in square brackets are open decisions.

## How to read the redline

- "Base" is the 2026-11-08 draft wording, quoted verbatim from the spec and copy deck. Where a clause had no base wording, the whole clause is shown as inserted.
- Our drafting aim is plain English that a buyer can read on a phone, that leans in the buyer's favour where the business can afford it, and that never lets the business keep money for a puppy it could not deliver.
- Nothing here is legal advice. Every statutory reference is for you to confirm or correct.

> **Note to counsel:** Base: spec §5 bullets 3 and 4. The base made the whole deposit non-refundable once the breeder is paid, which could exceed the business's actual loss. ## Part B. Delay clause

### B1. Estimated arrival

~~Arrival dates are estimates.~~<ins>Your reservation confirmation gives an estimated arrival date and the month it falls in. It is an estimate, not a fixed date.</ins> Puppies can fly to Singapore from 12 weeks old, and every import depends on AVS approval, the vet health certificate and an available flight.

<ins>Puppies fly at about 3 months old, so we work out the estimate from your puppy's date of birth: date of birth plus about 3 months. For example, if you reserve a puppy at 8 weeks old, it should arrive in about 4 weeks. At 6 weeks old, it should arrive in about 6 weeks.</ins>

<ins>If your puppy is already about 3 months old when you reserve, it flies once its paperwork and flight are ready, and your confirmation gives our estimate for that.</ins>

<ins>Once your confirmation is sent, the estimate in it does not change unless we tell you about a delay under B3. The 45-day and 90-day periods in B4 and B5 count from the end of the estimated month.</ins>

> **Note to counsel:** Base: copy deck §3 "About the arrival estimate" and FAQ "What if the arrival is late?" ("We give an estimated month, never a fixed date"). Revised 2026-11-13 (owner decision D6): the estimate is age-based, not a fixed number of weeks. Puppies fly at about 3 months old, so the estimate is the puppy's date of birth plus about 3 months, given as a date and its month in the confirmation. The earlier draft's minimum lead time is removed and is not promised. The worked example uses 12 weeks as the flying age. The sentence on a puppy already about 3 months old when reserved is ours, not the owners': the T17 answers do not cover that case, and the owners should confirm it. B4 and B5 still count from the end of the estimated month, the more generous reading for the buyer. Engineering note: the pipeline recalculates the estimate on every run, so the contractual month must be frozen in the confirmation and not taken from the live listing.

### B2. What can cause a delay

<ins>Delays can be caused by: AVS or the authorities in the puppy's home country taking longer to issue a licence or certificate; a vet advising that the puppy should wait before flying; airline embargoes, cancellations or seasonal restrictions on carrying animals; disease-control or other government restrictions; and changes to import rules.</ins>

> **Note to counsel:** New. This list describes what can happen. It does not limit the buyer's rights in B4 and B5. Revised 2026-11-13: "the Irish authorities" became "the authorities in the puppy's home country", so the clause also works for Australian reservations (Q13).

### B3. Keeping you informed

We'll keep you updated at every step on WhatsApp. <ins>Within 2 business days of learning of anything likely to push arrival past the estimated month, we will tell you the reason and give you a revised estimate.</ins>

> **Note to counsel:** Base: copy deck §3 "We'll keep you updated at every step on WhatsApp." The notice period is new.

### B4. Your options if arrival is late

<ins>If the puppy has not arrived by the last day of the estimated month, you may choose to keep waiting or to move your payment to another puppy.</ins> If ~~arrival slips more than 45 days past that month~~<ins>it has not arrived within 45 days after the end of the estimated month</ins>, you may <ins>also</ins> cancel for a full refund <ins>of everything you have paid, at any time before the puppy departs for Singapore. We pay the refund within 7 business days. This applies whatever the cause of the delay</ins>.

> **Note to counsel:** Base: copy deck §4 FAQ and spec §5 bullet 2 ("may cancel for a full refund"). Changes: the refund covers the balance; it applies whatever the cause; the right ends once the puppy is in the air, because a puppy in transit cannot practically be returned. Please confirm that cut-off is fair, or delete "at any time before the puppy departs for Singapore".

### B5. Long-stop date

<ins>If the puppy has not arrived within 90 days after the end of the estimated month, the reservation ends automatically and we refund everything you have paid, in full, within 7 business days, unless you have told us in writing that you want to keep waiting.</ins>

> **Note to counsel:** New. Without a long-stop date a buyer who stays silent could be left waiting indefinitely. Revised 2026-11-13: the owners have set the long-stop at 90 days.

### B6. If the import cannot go ahead

If your puppy can't come, ~~your deposit comes back to you~~<ins>clause 6 of the reservation terms applies and everything you have paid comes back to you</ins>.

> **Note to counsel:** Base: copy deck §3 "About the arrival estimate", last sentence. It mentioned only the deposit, but by this stage the buyer may also have paid the balance.

### B7. Events outside our control

<ins>We are not liable to pay compensation for a delay caused by events outside our reasonable control. This never affects your right to a refund under this Part or under clause 6.</ins>

> **Note to counsel:** New. This limits damages for delay but deliberately is not a force-majeure clause that would let the business keep money. Please advise whether excluding compensation passes the reasonableness test under the Unfair Contract Terms Act.

## Part B. Delay clause

**B1. Estimated arrival**

Your reservation confirmation gives an estimated arrival date and the month it falls in. It is an estimate, not a fixed date. Puppies can fly to Singapore from 12 weeks old, and every import depends on AVS approval, the vet health certificate and an available flight.

Puppies fly at about 3 months old, so we work out the estimate from your puppy's date of birth: date of birth plus about 3 months. For example, if you reserve a puppy at 8 weeks old, it should arrive in about 4 weeks. At 6 weeks old, it should arrive in about 6 weeks.

If your puppy is already about 3 months old when you reserve, it flies once its paperwork and flight are ready, and your confirmation gives our estimate for that.

Once your confirmation is sent, the estimate in it does not change unless we tell you about a delay under B3. The 45-day and 90-day periods in B4 and B5 count from the end of the estimated month.

**B2. What can cause a delay**

Delays can be caused by: AVS or the authorities in the puppy's home country taking longer to issue a licence or certificate; a vet advising that the puppy should wait before flying; airline embargoes, cancellations or seasonal restrictions on carrying animals; disease-control or other government restrictions; and changes to import rules.

**B3. Keeping you informed**

We'll keep you updated at every step on WhatsApp. Within 2 business days of learning of anything likely to push arrival past the estimated month, we will tell you the reason and give you a revised estimate.

**B4. Your options if arrival is late**

If the puppy has not arrived by the last day of the estimated month, you may choose to keep waiting or to move your payment to another puppy. If it has not arrived within 45 days after the end of the estimated month, you may also cancel for a full refund of everything you have paid, at any time before the puppy departs for Singapore. We pay the refund within 7 business days. This applies whatever the cause of the delay.

**B5. Long-stop date**

If the puppy has not arrived within 90 days after the end of the estimated month, the reservation ends automatically and we refund everything you have paid, in full, within 7 business days, unless you have told us in writing that you want to keep waiting.

**B6. If the import cannot go ahead**

If your puppy can't come, clause 6 of the reservation terms applies and everything you have paid comes back to you.

**B7. Events outside our control**

We are not liable to pay compensation for a delay caused by events outside our reasonable control. This never affects your right to a refund under this Part or under clause 6.
